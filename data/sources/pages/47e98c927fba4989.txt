Nuclear as a product, not a project.
Traditional nuclear treats each plant as a bespoke construction project, resulting in cost and schedule overruns.
Nuclear needs a new approach.
We call it an extra-modular reactor (XMR). The whole power plant, including electrical and mechanical systems, is factory-built into shipping container-sized modules.
On site, you connect fluid, power, and signal lines between modules. Then you fuel the core, load coolant, and commission the plant.
Construction takes days, not years.
We take the approach used by companies that mass produce hardware at civilizational scale: airplanes, cars, rockets.
‍
Design for manufacturing. Standardize modules. Build them in a factory. Ship them on trucks. Assemble on site. Then do it again, and again, and again.

Factory thesis
Most nuclear companies are design firms. They send their drawings to dozens of fabricators, and hope the parts arrive on time and fit together properly.
We chose a different path: welding our own vessels, building our own module scaffolding, assembling our own nuclear fuel. We write and develop our own software that controls the reactor and optimizes the factory. What we don't make comes from our best-in-class supplier network from across the West.
Case in point: the Critical Test Reactor was assembled in under four weeks. From design to operation in less than eight months. We didn't beat these deadlines through Powerpoints. We beat them by breaking problems down into solvable pieces, and building our own solutions.
