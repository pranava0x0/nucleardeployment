# Darlington New Nuclear Project

Building Canada and the G7's first Small Modular Reactor

【105†See update】

  * DNNP Update
## 【106†Small modular reactors】

OPG is building Canada's first grid-scale 【106†small modular reactor】 (SMR) to help meet Ontario's growing demand for reliable, clean power. Once completed, the Darlington New Nuclear Project (DNNP) will include four SMRs with a combined output of up to 1,200 megawatts - enough to power 1.2 million homes.
Construction of Unit 1 is progressing. In April 2026, OPG installed the 2.1-million-pound basemat - the first nuclear foundation laid in Canada in more than 30 years. Work is now underway on the reactor building and supporting infrastructure for the four planned SMRs.
DNNP is also helping Ontario build the skills and experience needed to deliver the next generation of nuclear power. The project is strengthening the province’s workforce, supply chain and engineering expertise while supporting 18,000 jobs during construction and 2,500 jobs once the units are operating.
These benefits extend to businesses and communities across Ontario. With 80% of project spending going to Ontario companies, DNNP will contribute $38.5 billion to the province’s GDP over 65 years. It is also helping Canadian suppliers build the capability to pursue future SMR opportunities at home and internationally.

## Project Update - Nov. 10, 2026
Construction is continuing on Canada and the G7's first small modular reactor. During the third quarter of 2026, the project completed assembly of the tunnel boring machine ahead of tunnelling activities expected to commence in the last quarter of 2026. The machine will bore and line the condenser cooling water tunnel, an essential element for bringing Canada’s first SMR online. The project also completed the first turbine building pour, marking a key milestone for the building foundation.
At the same time, OPG has identified pressures that will impact Unit 1’s cost and schedule. Based on the assessment completed to date, Unit 1 is no longer expected to meet the previously disclosed October 2030 commercial-operation target or be completed within the previously disclosed budget.
These pressures reflect ongoing engineering for this first-of-a-kind SMR and the resulting impacts on construction execution and supply chain performance. OPG is working closely with its technology provider, GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear Energy, to complete the outstanding engineering scope, establish an updated budget and schedule and move the project forward as quickly as possible.
This updated budget and schedule will be informed by a comprehensive assessment that is now underway and expected to be substantially complete by mid-2027. OPG will share further updates as additional information becomes available.
> We have made meaningful progress in building the G7’s first small modular reactor, and critical work continues every day on site. At the same time, we have identified risks that will impact Unit 1’s schedule and budget. As we continue construction, we are working to fully assess those impacts, and establish mitigation measures and a cost and schedule outlook that we can stand behind. As work progresses, we will share the information transparently.
>
> Nicolle Butcher

> President and CEO, OPG

## Questions & Answers

【110†Image】
##### Why do we need these SMRs in Ontario?

SMRs are a flexible, scalable nuclear solution. They can address climate change by helping Ontario manage increased electricity demand, stemming primarily from electrification of the transportation sector and other currently high-emitting sectors.
A fleet of four grid-scale SMRs in Ontario will also pave the way for the deployment of this technology elsewhere in Canada and abroad, particularly in those jurisdictions seeking to replace their coal-powered plants with carbon-free sources.
Nuclear is a proven carbon-free energy source. Canada will need to harness all clean energy technologies to achieve its climate change objectives and net-zero by 2050. Nuclear provides the clean, reliable baseload power necessary to meet increasing electricity demands.

SMR technologies are critical to this approach and represent significant potential for Canadian leadership.
The evidence is clear, as stated by experts like the International Atomic Energy Agency - without nuclear, there is no path to bringing the world’s carbon emissions to net-zero by 2050.

【111†Image】
##### What will the project cost?

The construction and operation and maintenance of four new nuclear Small Modular Reactor (SMR) plants will have a significant economic benefit for Ontario and Canada.

According to the Conference Board of Canada, deployment and operation of four SMR nuclear power plants is expected to increase Canadian GDP by $38.5 billion over 65 years. This includes $12.9 billion for the impact of construction and $25.6 billion for the impact of operation.
Over the next 65 years, the four-SMR nuclear plant assessed is expected to sustain, on average, approximately 3,700 jobs per year. This includes 18,000 jobs per year over the five years of construction and 2,500 jobs per year over the 60 years of operation.

The economic impact, or the ratio of increased GDP to spending (the “economic multiplier") is 0.91 – each dollar spent would increase Canadian GDP by $0.91 across the total lifespan of the project.
OPG is undertaking a comprehensive assessment of the project cost and schedule. The assessment will include an evaluation of engineering design maturity and completion timelines, first-of-a-kind design complexity impacts on construction and procurement assumptions, updated market conditions including inflationary and geopolitical risk, other residual risks, and the effectiveness of mitigation and recovery actions.
The assessment is currently expected to be substantially completed by mid-2027. OPG intends to provide further updates as additional material information becomes available.

【112†Image】
##### What’s the climate benefit?

Nuclear power is the backbone of Ontario’s electricity system and provides affordable and reliable energy 24/7 – avoiding 45 million tonnes of CO^{2} emissions each year.

In Canada alone, nuclear power helps avoid carbon emissions each year the equivalent of removing 15 million cars from the road.
A single SMR of about 300 Megawatts electric (MWe) in size can prevent between 0.3 megatonnes (MT) to 2 MT of CO^{2} emissions per year depending where it is located and what kind of power it is displacing.

Nuclear is also the right choice from a land-use perspective: to generate the same amount of energy as nuclear, solar would require about 100 times and wind about 500 times more land area.

##### Why not build another CANDU reactor like the existing Darlington units?
OPG recently invested in the Darlington Refurbishment project, and as 【114†announced in November 2025】, Pickering Nuclear now has the opportunity to continue producing safe, reliable energy for Ontario by delivering another world-class refurbishment project for the benefit of generations to come. Additionally, the provincial government announced in early 2025 it had directed OPG to explore opportunities for large-scale new nuclear generation at 【115†our Wesleyville site】 in Port Hope.
Large-scale nuclear technology forms the backbone of our nuclear generating fleet and will continue to do so for decades to come.

##### Is Ontario the only province looking at building SMRs?

No. The governments of Ontario, New Brunswick, Saskatchewan, and Alberta have signed a Memorandum of Understanding to collaborate to advance SMRs as a clean energy option to address climate change and regional energy demands, while supporting economic growth and innovation.
The 【118†SMR Feasibility Study】, formally requested as part of the MOU in December 2019, concludes that the development of SMRs would support domestic energy needs, curb greenhouse gas emissions, and position Canada as a global leader in this emerging technology.
Now, the four provinces have agreed to a 【119†joint strategic plan†www.ontario.ca】 outlining the path forward on SMRs. The plan builds on the SMR Feasibility Study by identifying provincial strategies for SMR deployment, including actions to enable decision making and actions following potential provincial decisions to proceed.

Beyond the original four-province MOU, in October 2025, Ontario and Nova Scotia signed a landmark agreement to collaborate on the development of SMRs.

##### Why should Ontario go first?

Ontario has a long history of safe, reliable nuclear power generation.

The province is also home to an extensive nuclear supply chain, which has been rejuvenated and recapitalized by refurbishment of existing nuclear generation facilities, including Darlington.
According to the Canadian Nuclear Association and Organization of Canadian Nuclear Industries, as of 2024, the nuclear industry contributes $22 billion per year to the Canadian GDP and supports about 89,000 jobs in Canada.

Not only that, but the Darlington site is the only site in Canada currently licensed for new nuclear build with an accepted environmental assessment.

Going first will benefit all Ontarians and cement Durham Region’s position as the clean energy capital of Ontario.
Acting as a first mover of SMR technology is a significant global leadership opportunity for Ontario and Canada. It will allow us to capture first-mover benefits and export market opportunities. It is likely companies involved in the first deployment will have opportunities elsewhere in Canada and abroad as use of SMRs grows.

##### How did you decide which technology partner to work with?

OPG conducted an extensive review process, starting in 2018, to examine the many different SMR technologies within the nuclear industry.
In 2020, we underwent a Due Diligence process, which included a deep dive into certain SMR technologies. From there, OPG developed a list of three developers who possessed technology we felt satisfied our environmental assessment commitments and could achieve our goal to have an SMR commercially available on the desired timeline.
Each technology was assessed against a number of key areas, such as: safety, technological readiness, licensability at our Darlington site, environmental impact, economic development potential, and cost.
In December 2021, OPG announced we were partnering with reactor technology developer, GE Hitachi Nuclear Energy (GEH). OPG is confident GEH’s BWRX-300 reactor technology is best positioned to provide a new source of carbon-free nuclear energy for Ontario’s future projected energy and capacity demand, and to support the potential for deployment of SMRs to other Canadian jurisdictions interested in reducing fossil fuel use.
