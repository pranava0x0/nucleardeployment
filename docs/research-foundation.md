# Research foundation

Status: MVP baseline
Verified through: 2026-07-17
Scope: public primary-source sample, not a complete census

## 1. Landscape summary

The current U.S. deployment story is not one fleet build. It is several parallel systems moving at different speeds:

1. Federal policy now sets explicit goals for reactor testing, military deployment, licensing reform, fuel, finance, workforce, and large-reactor construction.
2. DOE uses multiple pathways: cost-shared commercial demonstrations, test infrastructure at national laboratories, and DOE authorization for privately funded pilot reactors outside laboratories.
3. NRC licensing has moved beyond pre-application work for several advanced reactors. Hermes 2 has construction permits, Long Mott is under construction-permit review, and Natrium received a construction permit in March 2026.
4. Physical progress exists but remains concentrated. Natrium, Hermes 2, Project Pele, and fuel/test infrastructure provide stronger evidence than project announcements alone.
5. Zero-power criticality in the Reactor Pilot Program is meaningful technology evidence. It is not electricity generation, grid connection, commercial operation, or replication.
6. Fuel availability, qualified fabrication, licensing, project finance, customer contracts, and first-of-a-kind delivery remain cross-cutting gates.
7. Social and trade sources are discovery surfaces only. The retained source registry and search history live under `data/research/`.

The central product claim is therefore narrow: the United States is rebuilding the institutions and industrial pathways needed for deployment. The tracker must measure whether those pathways produce licensed, financed, constructed, operating, and repeated projects.

## 3. Executive-order crosswalk

### EO 14299 — Deploying Advanced Nuclear Reactor Technologies for National Security

- Primary goal: advanced reactors for military, national-security, AI, and critical infrastructure needs.
- Key measurable deliverable: Army-regulated reactor operating at a domestic military installation by September 30, 2028.
- Other gates: DOE site designation, private reactor authorization at DOE sites, HALEU fuel bank, interagency agreements, and export coordination.
- Tracker test: a named site, procurement pathway, selected design, authorization basis, construction milestones, and operation—not the order alone.
- Source: <https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/05/deploying-advanced-nuclear-reactor-technologies-for-national-security/>

### EO 14300 — Ordering the Reform of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission

- Primary goal: revise NRC structure, rules, guidance, fees, and review schedules.
- Key measurable deliverable: fixed licensing deadlines, including a maximum 18-month decision target for new-reactor applications, subject to applicant performance and implementing rules.
- Other gates: rules for tested DOE/DoD designs, high-volume microreactor/modular licensing, radiation standards, hearings, and oversight.
- Tracker test: published rule or guidance, effective date, docket behavior, actual review duration, and court or statutory constraints.
- Source: <https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/05/ordering-the-reform-of-the-nuclear-regulatory-commission/>

### EO 14301 — Reforming Nuclear Reactor Testing at the Department of Energy

- Primary goal: accelerate DOE-authorized reactor testing.
- Key measurable deliverable: pilot pathway for privately funded reactors outside national laboratories, with at least three targeted criticalities by July 4, 2026.
- Program link: Reactor Pilot Program and later Launch Pad activity.
- Tracker test: documented safety authorization, construction, fuel loading, criticality, test results, and conversion to a commercial licensing basis.
- Source: <https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/05/reforming-nuclear-reactor-testing-at-the-department-of-energy/>

### EO 14302 — Reinvigorating the Nuclear Industrial Base

- Primary goal: rebuild fuel, supply chain, workforce, finance, and reactor-delivery capacity.
- Key measurable deliverables: fuel and supply-chain actions, financing activity, workforce programs, and ten large reactor designs under construction by 2030.
- Tracker test: closed financing, issued procurement, factory capacity, qualified suppliers, awarded contracts, construction authorization, and physical work.
- Source: <https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/05/reinvigorating-the-nuclear-industrial-base/>

## 5. Unified deployment framework

The public eight-stage deployment pipeline is a navigation layer. It does not replace the five detailed maturity tracks.

### Public deployment stages

1. Interest
2. Preliminary
3. Development
4. Contractual
5. Ready
6. Physical
7. Operational
8. Replication

### Detailed maturity tracks

- Technology: concept → detailed design → component/fuel validation → integrated test → criticality → power operation → repeat design.
- Regulation: no engagement → pre-application → application → accepted review → review/RAIs → permit/license → conditions → fuel/operation authorization → oversight.
- Commercial: interest → MOU/LOI → reservation → site/FEED → binding development → offtake/equipment order → notice to proceed → fleet/operating customer.
- Finance: venture/strategic capital → federal research/demonstration support → conditional loan → closed loan/equity/debt → final investment decision → financial close → construction draws → operating refinance.
- Delivery: candidate site → site control → environmental/permitting → engineering/procurement → early work → construction → equipment/mechanical completion → fuel → criticality/testing → electricity/grid/full power → commercial operation → repeat unit.

One project may be advanced on one track and early on another. Public pages show a compact summary; the future normalized schema stores all five separately.

## 6. Technology taxonomy — first tranche

Every record separates four axes:

- generation: Gen II, Gen III+, Gen IV, or experimental / unclassified
- scale: large reactor, SMR, microreactor, test reactor, or critical experiment
- reactor family: LWR/PWR, LWR/BWR, HTGR, SFR, FHR, MSR, heat-pipe, or design TBD
- role: commercial power, demonstration power, research/test, critical experiment, or site envelope

These axes must not be collapsed. An SMR can be a PWR or BWR; a microreactor can be gas-cooled, heat-pipe, fast-spectrum, or undisclosed.

| Technology | Typical role | Fuel / coolant | Meaningful validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Large LWR / Gen III+ | Utility electricity | Low-enriched uranium / water | Repeated construction with cost and schedule control |
| Sodium fast reactor | Electricity plus thermal storage | HALEU metal fuel / liquid sodium | Licensed construction, operation, full power, and repeat order |
| High-temperature gas reactor | Electricity and industrial heat | TRISO / helium | Qualified fuel at scale and sustained commercial heat/power |
| Fluoride salt-cooled high-temperature reactor | Test then commercial heat/power | TRISO pebbles / fluoride salt | Hermes data followed by licensed power operation |
| Microreactor | Remote, defense, industrial, resilience | Design-dependent / design-dependent | Transport, installation, authorization, multi-year reliable operation |
| Molten-salt reactor | Heat/electricity and fuel-cycle variants | Salt fuel or solid fuel / salt | Materials, chemistry, fuel qualification, and integrated operation |
| Lead-cooled fast reactor | Electricity and industrial use | Fast-spectrum fuel / lead | Materials, coolant handling, licensed demonstration, power operation |

## 7. Fuel and supply-chain taxonomy

Upstream fuel:

- uranium mining
- conversion
- LEU and HALEU enrichment
- deconversion
- fuel fabrication
- TRISO particles and pebbles
- qualification and irradiation testing

Heavy industrial base:

- forgings and vessels
- pumps, valves, heat exchangers
- specialty alloys, graphite, salt, sodium, zirconium
- instrumentation and control
- nuclear-grade concrete
- modular manufacturing
- qualified suppliers and nuclear QA programs

Delivery base:

- engineering and EPC capacity
- craft workforce
- transportation
- site preparation
- commissioning
- waste and used-fuel handling

Each future dependency record needs supplier/facility, capacity, qualification status, linked projects, alternative source, schedule risk, and primary evidence.

## 12. Source and verification method

1. Start with the named primary record: regulator docket, agency program page, order, statute, award, filing, or executed agreement.
2. Record the claim as narrowly as the source supports it.
3. Label government-reported, company-reported, third-party-reported, or independently verified.
4. Separate announcement date, expected date, and actual completion date.
5. Record a superseding or terminating event instead of overwriting history.
6. Show conflicting schedules and identify which source is newer.
7. Run link liveness, but do not treat a live link as proof of accuracy.
8. Require manual editorial review before publication.

- Weekly: review NRC advanced-reactor highlights and active applicant dashboards; DOE program and financing updates.
- Monthly: review executive-order deliverables, award status, program milestones, project schedules, and dead links.
- Quarterly: capital transactions, valuations, utility commitments, fuel/supply-chain capacity, international comparisons.

## 16. Major gaps and unresolved questions

- Complete denominator for U.S. projects and reactor units.
- Distinction between the 11 pilot projects and the number of participating companies.
- Uniform rule for non-NRC DOE/DoD authorization milestones versus NRC commercial licensing.
- Treatment of enabling construction before a nuclear construction permit.
- Boundaries between a critical experiment, test reactor, demonstration reactor, and commercial power reactor.
- Company-reported schedule confidence when the regulator has no corresponding milestone.
- Project-level allocation of broad federal appropriations and supply-chain loans.
- Global comparison source hierarchy and country-by-country regulator coverage.
- Confidential private financing and undisclosed binding terms.
- Rules for stale projects with no public update for more than two years.

These gaps block comprehensive national/global totals. They do not block the small evidence-led MVP.
