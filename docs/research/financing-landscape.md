## Cost benchmarks by reactor class

### Large LWR (the only class with U.S. actuals)

- Lazard prices U.S. new-build nuclear at $141–220/MWh unsubsidized, with a $169/MWh marker for the Vogtle 3&4 actual, on assumptions of ~2.2 GW, ~$32.3B total capital cost, ~97% capacity factor, 70-year life, and a ~30% cost learning between Vogtle unit 3 and unit 4. [report, p. 8] <https://www.lazard.com/media/eijnqja3/lazards-lcoeplus-june-2025.pdf>
- Eash-Gates et al.: "A survey of plants begun after 1970 shows an average overnight cost overrun of 241%," and nth-of-a-kind U.S. plants have historically been more, not less, expensive than first-of-a-kind. At writing (2020) Vogtle was estimated at $25B ($11,000/kW) against an initial $14B. [report, p. 1] <https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/133049/Jacopo's%20and%20Jessika's%20paper%20on%20nuclear%20cost%20Sep%202020.pdf>
- DOE Liftoff (Sept 2024 update) prices new nuclear LCOE at $61–122/MWh with the 30% 48E ITC, on overnight capital of $7,000–20,000/kW and 6-year construction; the ranges are "early of a kind," between FOAK and NOAK. [report, p. 14] <https://gain.inl.gov/content/uploads/4/2024/11/DOE-Advanced-Nuclear-Liftoff-Report.pdf>

### SMR

- DOE Liftoff: a committed orderbook of 5–10 deployments of at least one reactor design is the first essential step to liftoff (p. 6); "cost reductions of ~45-60% are estimated between the first and third plant deployed of a given reactor concept" (p. 35); a consortium with a committed orderbook of 10+ units "can significantly reduce the financial risks involved, even helping to negate the effect of cost overruns from the first few plants," and ordering in bulk could cut ~15% from the first unit (p. 37). [report] <https://gain.inl.gov/content/uploads/4/2024/11/DOE-Advanced-Nuclear-Liftoff-Report.pdf>
- Liftoff on absolute exposure: a $4B SMR with a 50% overrun completes at $6B; a $10B large reactor with the same 50% overrun completes at $15B — the smaller unit caps the absolute loss (p. 30). [report] <https://gain.inl.gov/content/uploads/4/2024/11/DOE-Advanced-Nuclear-Liftoff-Report.pdf>
- Real-world FOAK SMR price history: the January 2023 NuScale 8-K states the CFPP's "updated target price of $89 per megawatt hour" [gov, SEC 8-K] <https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1822966/000182296623000008/pressreleasenuscalereach.htm>; NRECA's termination advisory records the target-price rise from $55/MWh to $89/MWh and the estimated construction-cost growth from $5.3B to $9.3B before the 2023-11-08 termination for lack of subscription [press] <https://www.cooperative.com/programs-services/bts/documents/advisories/advisory-nuscale-and-uamps-end-smr-project-cfpp-dec-2023.pdf>; the termination itself is in the joint 8-K [gov, SEC 8-K] <https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1822966/000182296623000256/uampsnuscalejointpressre.htm>
- Real-world FOAK SMR budget (Canada): OPG's approved Darlington plan puts the first BWRX-300 at a combined C$7.7B (C$6.1B reactor + C$1.6B systems common to all four units) and the four-unit 1,200 MW project at C$20.9B in 2024 dollars. [press] <https://www.powermag.com/ontario-authorizes-opg-to-start-construction-of-first-commercial-nuclear-smr/>
- TVA has estimated the first Clinch River BWRX-300 at about $5.4B before interest costs and tax credits. [press] <https://www.wkms.org/energy/2025-10-14/nuclear-hype-is-building-tva-plans-to-buy-in>
- TerraPower's stated all-in cost for Kemmerer Unit 1 is roughly $4B, split ~50/50 with DOE under ARDP — a figure the company has held since 2021. [press] <https://www.powermag.com/terrapowers-kemmerer-1-enters-construction-timeline-of-the-natrium-projects-road-to-first-power/>

### Microreactor

- INL bottom-up estimate (INL/RPT-24-80433, LMTR-20 reference design): FOAK overnight cost ~$14,500/kWe excluding fuel (~$19,800/kWe with fuel; total capital investment >$22,000/kWe with interest), giving a FOAK LCOE of $325/MWh (pp. 5, 61); with factory production, an NOAK microreactor could reach ~$6,000/kWe overnight and LCOE around ~$120/MWh (p. 5). [report] <https://gain.inl.gov/content/uploads/4/2025/03/2024-Document-INL-RPT-24-80433.pdf>
- NEI (2019): FOAK microreactors could generate at $0.14–0.41/kWh, overlapping remote diesel generation costs — the first market where micro FOAK economics close. [press] <https://www.nucnet.org/news/electricity-from-micro-reactors-will-cost-less-than-diesel-generators-says-nei-report>
- DOE Liftoff: microreactors need an orderbook of ~30-50 reactors to underpin factory production, versus ~5-10 for SMRs or large reactors (p. 31). [report] <https://gain.inl.gov/content/uploads/4/2024/11/DOE-Advanced-Nuclear-Liftoff-Report.pdf>
- Company cost targets on record: "Oklo expects its plants' levelized electricity costs to range from $40/MWh to $90/MWh, according to its shareholder presentation." [press] <https://www.utilitydive.com/news/oklo-advanced-nuclear-microreactor-project-pipeline-nrc/724343/> Deep Fission and Endeavour target 5–7 cents/kWh for the 2 GW data-center framework. [press] <https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/deep-fission-and-endeavour-announce-strategic-partnership>
- Government FOAK actual: the Project Pele prototype (1.5 MWe transportable) is a cost-type DOD contract of approximately $300M. [press] <https://www.powermag.com/dod-picks-bwxt-to-manufacture-project-pele-prototype-nuclear-microreactor/>

## Cost overruns: history and expected range

- Average U.S. overnight cost overrun for plants begun after 1970: 241% (Eash-Gates, p. 1, above). Vogtle finished around twice its initial estimate (Lazard's ~$32.3B capital assumption vs. the $14B initial; p. 8 above). V.C. Summer was abandoned in 2017 with $9B sunk (Eash-Gates, p. 1).
- UAMPS/NuScale CFPP: +53% target-price growth ($55→$89/MWh, on a 75% construction-cost jump from $5.3B to $9.3B) before construction ever started (NRECA advisory above) — pre-construction escalation is part of the observed range, not just build-phase overrun.
- Liftoff frames the 30% ITC as de facto overrun insurance: "the ITC applies 30-50% to capital cost regardless of initial budget, so in effect provides 'overrun insurance'" (pp. 8, 71). [report] <https://gain.inl.gov/content/uploads/4/2024/11/DOE-Advanced-Nuclear-Liftoff-Report.pdf>
- Historical driver mix: soft factors external to reactor hardware contributed over half the 1976–1987 cost rise; rework elimination, standardization, and constructor proficiency dominate the modeled FOAK→NOAK levers (Liftoff pp. 35-36; Eash-Gates).

## Mechanisms proposed but not yet law or practice

- ARC Act (introduced 2026-02-10, Risch/Gallego): a risk reduction program creating "safeguards against unforeseen costs through enhanced financing terms and a limited federal cost share," to benefit three or more advanced nuclear projects. [gov] <https://www.risch.senate.gov/news/press-releases/risch-gallego-introduce-bill-to-accelerate-new-nuclear-investment/>
- CATF proposal: a federal cost-overrun insurance facility with tiered risk-sharing among sponsors, states, and the federal government, gated on competitive selection, proven integrated project delivery, a paid premium, and state support. [press/NGO] <https://www.catf.us/2025/05/can-they-make-atoms-great-again/>

## Insurance and liability pooling (in force)

- Price-Anderson: two-layer pooled liability, extended to 2065 for new reactors. Each reactor carries primary insurance; above it, every covered reactor owes retrospective premiums of up to $165.9M per reactor per incident (including the 5% surcharge), paid at up to ~$24.7M/year. [gov, CRS] <https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10821>
- NEIL: the industry's mutual insurer for property/decontamination/business interruption; PG&E's 2025 annual report describes up to $3.2B per incident in NEIL property coverage. [gov, SEC ARS] <https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1004980/000100498026000024/a2025annualreportmaster.htm>

## Siting and regulatory economics by class

- NRC's 2023 emergency-preparedness final rule (effective 2023-12-18) lets SMRs and other new technologies set a scalable, consequence-based EPZ — the zone within which projected dose exceeds 10 mSv over 96 hours — so a small source term can put the EPZ at the site boundary. [gov] <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/11/16/2023-25163/emergency-preparedness-for-small-modular-reactors-and-other-new-technologies>
- ADVANCE Act fee relief: NRC's advanced-reactor applicant hourly rate is $148 versus the $318 full-cost rate, from FY2025. [press] <https://www.ans.org/news/2025-06-25/article-7136/nrc-cuts-50-percent-off-for-advanced-reactor-applicants/>
- Part 53, the optional risk-informed, technology-inclusive licensing framework, was finalized 2026-03-26. [press] <https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/nrc-finalizes-new-optional-licensing-framework-for-advanced-reactors/>
- Footprints on record: eVinci sites on as little as two acres [press] <https://www.powermag.com/westinghouse-secures-first-customer-for-evinci-nuclear-microreactor/>; KRONOS MMR under five acres per unit [company] <https://nanonuclearenergy.com/kronos-mmr/>; IMSR about 17 acres with a ~130m x 145m security perimeter [gov] <https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2009/ML20097B839.pdf>; Deep Fission emplaces the reactor a mile underground in a ~30-inch borehole [company] <https://www.deepfission.com/technology>; Last Energy plans up to 30 units on 200 acres [press] <https://www.utilitydive.com/news/last-energy-microreactors-texas-ercot-data-centers/741268/>. Vogtle's two AP1000s represent the large-LWR envelope: ~2.2 GW and ~$32.3B on Lazard's assumptions (p. 8 above).
- DOE authorization (Reactor Pilot Program, DOME/NRIC test beds) substitutes for NRC licensing only on DOE-controlled test projects; every commercial sale still requires an NRC license, which is why the pilot criticalities contribute 0 MWe to the race bands.
